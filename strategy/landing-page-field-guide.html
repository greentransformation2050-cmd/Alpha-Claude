Sample Entries

Fig. 10 — Relationships & Dating

Fawning

What it is

A trauma-response pattern where keeping the peace — including over-apologizing — becomes a learned way to stay safe, not genuine politeness.

Try this

Swap the reflexive apology for a neutral response: "No worries — let's reschedule." No apology required for someone else's choice.

Fig. 17 — Work, Money & Negotiation

Anchoring Bias

What it is

The first number mentioned in a negotiation sets the mental range for everything that follows, even when it shouldn't.

Try this

Where possible, let the other side anchor first — "what's the budget for this role?" — and negotiate up from their number.

Fig. 2 — Understanding Yourself

Sunk Cost Fallacy

What it is

We keep investing in something because we've already invested in it — even when continuing is the worse choice.

Try this

Ask: "If I were starting today, knowing what I know now, would I choose this?" The sunk cost is gone either way.
