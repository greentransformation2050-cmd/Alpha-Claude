## Shorts — remaining 15 full scripts

### 2. Mere-Exposure Effect
- **Hook (0-2s):** "Why do you end up liking people you couldn't stand at first?"
- **Escalation (3-15s):** "New coworker. Week one, mildly annoying. Week six, you're grabbing lunch together. Nothing about them changed."
- **Twist (15-30s):** "It's the mere-exposure effect — your brain treats familiarity as safety. The more you see something, the more your brain quietly relabels it as 'good,' whether or not it actually is."
- **Payoff (30-40s):** "Use it on purpose: if you want to build a relationship or a habit, proximity beats intensity. Small, repeated contact does more than one big gesture."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Who fits this for you — someone you now love who you couldn't stand at first?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to the friend who married their 'I can't stand them' coworker."

### 3. IKEA Effect
- **Hook:** "Why you love things you built more than things you bought — even when they're worse."
- **Escalation:** "Your wobbly bookshelf you assembled at 11pm with the wrong screws? You defend it like it's furniture royalty."
- **Twist:** "It's called the IKEA Effect — effort creates ownership, and ownership inflates value in your own head, disproportionate to actual quality."
- **Payoff:** "Use it with teams and kids: let people build a small piece themselves. Buy-in isn't persuaded, it's built."
- **Comment Trigger:** "What's your IKEA Effect object — the ugly thing you built and now love?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag someone who's way too proud of their DIY disaster."

### 4. Pratfall Effect
- **Hook:** "Why competent people become MORE likeable after they mess up."
- **Escalation:** "The polished coworker who never slips up? Respected. The one who trips, laughs at themselves, and keeps going? Loved."
- **Twist:** "The Pratfall Effect: a small, visible mistake makes already-competent people seem more human and approachable — but it backfires on people who haven't shown competence first."
- **Payoff:** "Don't hide every flaw. If you're already seen as capable, a well-handled stumble builds trust, not doubt."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Ever noticed this — who's the 'lovable mess' at your job?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to the perfectionist who's afraid to look human at work."

### 5. Reciprocity
- **Hook:** "The 20-second trick that gets people to say yes."
- **Escalation:** "Free sample at the store. You didn't ask for it. Suddenly buying the jar feels less like a sale and more like... fair."
- **Twist:** "Reciprocity — humans are wired to repay what they receive, even small, unsolicited things. It's one of the oldest, most reliable levers in influence."
- **Payoff:** "Ethical version: give real value first, expect nothing, and let goodwill build naturally — don't weaponize it as manufactured guilt."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Where has a 'free sample' gotten you before?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to someone who works in sales — they'll recognize it instantly."

### 8. Fundamental Attribution Error
- **Hook:** "Why you think you're 'having a bad day' but they're just 'a bad person.'"
- **Escalation:** "Someone cuts you off in traffic — 'what a jerk.' You cut someone off — 'I was late, it's not usually like me.'"
- **Twist:** "Fundamental attribution error: we explain our own behavior by circumstance, and everyone else's behavior by character. Same action, opposite explanation, depending on who did it."
- **Payoff:** "Next time someone frustrates you, ask: 'what circumstance might explain this, the way I'd explain my own bad moment?'"
- **Comment Trigger:** "Who deserves that same grace from you today?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag the friend who's hardest on other people and softest on themselves."

### 9. Loss Aversion
- **Hook:** "Why losing $20 hurts more than finding $20 feels good."
- **Escalation:** "Same $20, opposite direction. One ruins your afternoon. The other you forget by dinner."
- **Twist:** "Loss aversion — psychologically, losses hit about twice as hard as equivalent gains. Your brain isn't rational about money, it's protective."
- **Payoff:** "This is why 'limited time' pricing works on you, and why you hold bad investments too long. Naming the bias breaks its grip on the decision."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Does this explain your last impulse buy or bad hold?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to whoever's still 'waiting for it to bounce back.'"

### 10. Spotlight Effect
- **Hook:** "Nobody remembers your embarrassing moment as much as you think."
- **Escalation:** "You replay that one comment from three years ago on a loop. The person who heard it? Doesn't remember it happened."
- **Twist:** "Spotlight effect — we wildly overestimate how much other people notice and remember about us, because we're the main character in our own head, not theirs."
- **Payoff:** "The math is genuinely on your side here: most people are too busy running their own spotlight effect to be running yours."
- **Comment Trigger:** "What moment are you still replaying that they've probably forgotten?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag someone who needs to let an old embarrassing moment go."

### 12. Sunk Cost Fallacy
- **Hook:** "Why you finish bad movies and stay in bad relationships."
- **Escalation:** "Two hours into a movie you hate. 'I've already sat through half of it' — so you sit through the rest."
- **Twist:** "Sunk cost fallacy: we keep investing in something specifically because we've already invested, even when the smart move is to stop. Past cost isn't a reason to add future cost."
- **Payoff:** "The question that breaks it: 'if I were starting today, knowing what I know now, would I choose this?' If no, the sunk cost is gone either way."
- **Comment Trigger:** "What are you finishing just because you started it?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to whoever's still 'giving it one more chance' out of obligation."

### 13. Social Proof
- **Hook:** "Why empty restaurants feel scarier than crowded ones — even with better food."
- **Escalation:** "Two restaurants, same block. One's packed, one's empty. You don't even check the menus — you just walk into the crowd."
- **Twist:** "Social proof: when we're uncertain, we outsource the decision to what everyone else is doing, assuming the crowd knows something we don't. Often it doesn't."
- **Payoff:** "Use it as a check, not a crutch: ask *why* the crowd is there before joining it. Sometimes it's quality. Sometimes it's just... a crowd."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Name a time you followed the crowd and regretted it."
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag someone who always picks the busy restaurant."

### 14. Dunning-Kruger Curve
- **Hook:** "The least skilled people are the most confident. Here's the actual chart."
- **Escalation:** "Beginner at anything — chess, cooking, public speaking — early confidence is sky-high, right before it should be."
- **Twist:** "The Dunning-Kruger curve: low competence often comes with low ability to *recognize* low competence, so confidence peaks early, then crashes as real skill (and real awareness of the gap) grows."
- **Payoff:** "Rising doubt after you start learning something isn't a bad sign — it's usually proof you're actually getting more accurate about the size of the topic."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Where are you on the curve right now, honestly?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to your most confidently-wrong group chat friend — lovingly."

### 15. Cognitive Dissonance
- **Hook:** "Why you defend decisions you secretly know were wrong."
- **Escalation:** "Bought the expensive thing. Deep down, regret. Out loud: 'no, it was actually worth it, here's why...'"
- **Twist:** "Cognitive dissonance — holding two conflicting beliefs (I'm smart / I made a dumb choice) is so uncomfortable that your brain edits the story to make it consistent, usually by defending the choice instead of admitting the mistake."
- **Payoff:** "Catching it in real time is the skill: notice the urge to over-explain a decision, and ask what you're actually protecting — usually it's not the decision, it's your self-image."
- **Comment Trigger:** "What decision are you still defending a little too hard?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag whoever's still justifying that purchase."

### 16. Bystander Effect
- **Hook:** "Why more witnesses means less help — the math is genuinely worrying."
- **Escalation:** "Someone collapses on a busy street. Counterintuitively, they're statistically *less* likely to get fast help than if only one person had seen it."
- **Twist:** "Bystander effect — responsibility diffuses across a crowd. Everyone assumes someone else will act, so often nobody does."
- **Payoff:** "The fix is specific: if you ever need help in public, don't shout 'somebody help' — point at one person and say 'you, in the blue jacket, call an ambulance.' Naming someone collapses the diffusion instantly."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Did you know this before today?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Save this — it's genuinely useful information to have ready."

### 17. Confirmation Bias
- **Hook:** "Why arguing with facts never changes anyone's mind."
- **Escalation:** "You send someone a solid source. They don't update their view — they find a reason to dismiss the source instead."
- **Twist:** "Confirmation bias: we don't process new information neutrally. We hunt for evidence that supports what we already believe, and scrutinize (or ignore) anything that doesn't."
- **Payoff:** "You can't out-fact a feeling. What actually moves people: curiosity-first questions ('what would change your mind?') land where direct contradiction bounces off."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Where do you catch yourself doing this?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this before your next family group chat debate."

### 18. Benjamin Franklin Effect
- **Hook:** "Ask someone for a favor if you want them to like you. Here's why that works."
- **Escalation:** "Backwards logic: doing someone a favor *for* them should make them like you more. Turns out, asking *them* for a small favor can work even better."
- **Twist:** "The Benjamin Franklin effect — when we do something kind for someone, our brain looks for a reason why, and lands on 'I must like them.' The favor creates the fondness, not the other way around."
- **Payoff:** "Small, genuine asks ('can I get your opinion on this?') build real rapport — bigger than most people expect from such a small interaction."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Have you ever felt closer to someone after helping them, not the reverse?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag someone you want to get closer to — then actually try this."

### 19. Parasocial Relationships
- **Hook:** "Why you feel like you 'know' a creator you've never met."
- **Escalation:** "You've watched someone talk to a camera for a year. You know their dog's name, their coffee order, their voice when they're tired. They don't know you exist."
- **Twist:** "Parasocial relationship — a one-sided bond that *feels* mutual because your brain processes repeated, intimate-feeling exposure the same way it processes a real friendship, even though there's no actual relationship."
- **Payoff:** "Not inherently unhealthy — it becomes a problem only when it replaces real reciprocal connection instead of supplementing it. Worth noticing which one it is for you."
- **Comment Trigger:** "Which creator does this apply to for you? Be honest."
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag the friend who talks about their favorite YouTuber like a close friend."

## Long-form — remaining 7 outlines (8-12 min)

### 2. "Why We Lie to Ourselves: The Psychology of Self-Deception"
- **Hook (0-20s):** Cold open: three quick scenes of small self-deceptions (defending an ex, justifying a skipped workout, explaining away a red flag) — "None of these are dishonesty toward others. They're dishonesty toward yourself — and it's more common than lying to anyone else."
- **Escalation (20s-2min):** Set premise — self-deception isn't a character flaw, it's a documented cognitive strategy your brain uses to protect self-image and reduce anxiety.
- **Body (2-9min):** Segments on cognitive dissonance reduction, motivated reasoning, the "better-than-average" effect, and denial as an emotional buffer — each with a real, relatable scenario and the named mechanism.
- **Twist (9-10min):** Show how these combine into one real pattern (e.g., staying in a job/relationship past its expiry) — the "self-deception stack."
- **Payoff (10-11min):** One honest self-check question: "What would I tell a friend in my exact situation?" — the gap between that answer and your own belief is usually where the self-deception lives.
- **Comment Trigger:** "What's one thing you're maybe not being fully honest with yourself about?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to someone who needs permission to admit they were wrong about something."

### 3. "The Science of First Impressions (And How to Actually Improve Yours)"
- **Hook:** "You get judged in under a tenth of a second — before you've said a single word. Here's what's actually being measured."
- **Escalation:** Explain thin-slicing research — brief exposure judgments correlate surprisingly well with longer-term impressions, for better or worse.
- **Body:** Segments on the halo effect, warmth-vs-competence framework (people judge you on both axes almost instantly), primacy effect, and the specific, controllable levers (posture, vocal tone, first-sentence content) backed by real research, not folklore.
- **Twist:** Reveal that "confidence" as usually taught is often actually "perceived warmth" — likability moves people more than dominance in most everyday contexts.
- **Payoff:** A 3-step, practical framework for the first 30 seconds of any interaction.
- **Comment Trigger:** "Which do you lead with — warmth or competence? Which do you think you should?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to someone with an interview or first date coming up."

### 5. "The Psychology of Manipulation — And How to Spot It Before It Works on You"
- **Hook:** "Every manipulation tactic follows one of about six patterns. Once you can name them, they stop working on you as easily."
- **Escalation:** Frame explicitly as protective/defensive content — the goal is recognition, not replication — stated clearly up front.
- **Body:** Segments on gaslighting, love-bombing, DARVO (deny-attack-reverse victim-offender), guilt-tripping, and false urgency — each with the real mechanism, a realistic (not sensationalized) example, and a concrete recognition cue.
- **Twist:** Show that manipulation tactics are often used unconsciously by people who aren't malicious, not just by "bad actors" — nuance that avoids painting all instances as intentional abuse while still taking real harm seriously.
- **Payoff:** A simple gut-check script viewers can use in the moment: "Am I being asked to doubt something I clearly saw/heard/felt?"
- **Comment Trigger:** "Which pattern have you experienced? (No need to name names.)"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to someone who needs the language for what they're experiencing."

### 6. "Why Money Doesn't Make You Happy (The Real Research)"
- **Hook:** "There's a real number where more money stops making you happier day-to-day. Here's what the actual research found — and it's not what headlines say."
- **Escalation:** Walk through hedonic adaptation — how quickly humans re-baseline to new circumstances, good or bad.
- **Body:** Segments on the income-happiness plateau research, lifestyle inflation, and the specific spending categories (experiences, time-buying, giving) that reliably outperform stuff-buying for well-being.
- **Twist:** Reframe — the real lever isn't income size, it's the *gap* between income and lifestyle inflation; a modest earner with no inflation can out-happy a high earner with heavy inflation.
- **Payoff:** Three research-backed spending shifts viewers can make this month without earning more.
- **Comment Trigger:** "What's one purchase that actually made you happier long-term, not just in the moment?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to someone chasing a raise as the whole plan."

### 7. "The Real Reason You Procrastinate (It's Not Laziness)"
- **Hook:** "Procrastination has almost nothing to do with time management. It's an emotion-regulation problem wearing a productivity costume."
- **Escalation:** Debunk the "just use a planner" folk theory with the actual research: procrastination correlates more with mood-repair avoidance than with poor scheduling.
- **Body:** Segments on temporal discounting (future-you feels like a stranger), task-aversiveness and emotional avoidance, and the "fresh start effect" as a real, usable trigger.
- **Twist:** Reveal that self-criticism after procrastinating *increases* future procrastination (guilt-avoidance loop) — the thing that feels like accountability is often making it worse.
- **Payoff:** A concrete 2-minute-start technique plus self-forgiveness research finding that actually reduces repeat procrastination.
- **Comment Trigger:** "What's the task you're procrastinating on literally right now?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag your favorite productive-on-the-outside, drowning-on-the-inside friend."

### 9. "Why Group Chats Turn Toxic: The Psychology of Online Groups"
- **Hook:** "Take five perfectly reasonable people, put them in a group chat, and watch what happens to how they communicate. It's predictable — and it has a name."
- **Escalation:** Introduce deindividuation — reduced personal accountability in group/anonymous-feeling settings.
- **Body:** Segments on groupthink (pressure toward consensus over accuracy), online disinhibition effect (distance lowers social filters), and pile-on dynamics (diffusion of responsibility applied to conflict, not just inaction).
- **Twist:** Show that the same person behaves differently 1-on-1 vs. in the group — it's the structure, not individual character, driving most of the toxicity.
- **Payoff:** Three concrete norms that measurably reduce group toxicity (named accountability, slowing response speed, moving heated topics to 1-on-1).
- **Comment Trigger:** "Has your personality changed in a group chat vs. one-on-one with the same people?"
- **Share Trigger:** "Send this to the group chat that needs to see it (yes, that one)."

### 10. "The Attachment Styles Explained: Which One Are You (Really)?"
- **Hook:** "There are four attachment styles, and one of them is probably running your relationships without your permission."
- **Escalation:** Brief, respectful overview of attachment theory's origin (early caregiver bonds) and its adult-relationship echo, with a clear non-diagnostic framing.
- **Body:** Segments on secure, anxious, avoidant, and disorganized styles — each with a realistic relational example (texting patterns, conflict response, closeness comfort) rather than stereotypes.
- **Twist:** Clarify that attachment style is a tendency, not a life sentence — "earned security" is a real, researched outcome of consistent healthy relationships/self-work.
- **Payoff:** Point viewers to the channel's own downloadable self-assessment (Field Guide cross-sell) for a deeper, private look rather than a 30-second guess.
- **Comment Trigger:** "Which one do you suspect you are — no wrong answers."
- **Share Trigger:** "Tag the person you'd want to understand this about (with love, not blame)."
